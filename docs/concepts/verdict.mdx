## Candidates

A **candidate** is one state of the code, identified by its git tree. Each commit that an implementer makes is a candidate.
The baseline that `piloop init` measures is the first one.

Because a candidate is a tree, a commit that goes back to a known state is that known candidate, and piloop does not
measure it again. It keeps its verdict, unless another run reaches it: then the daemon's verdict, if it is not a keep,
is judged again on that run's bar. A keep stays. A run's commits are its first-parent history, so a line that an implementer
merges in is not the run's, and a merge's parent is the commit it merged onto. A candidate can have several
measurements. Its score is their mean under the current
[evaluator](/concepts/campaigns#evaluators).

## The head and the bar

The **head** is the latest kept candidate on the trunk. New runs start from it, unless an idea pins another starting
point.

Each run is judged against its **bar**. The bar starts at the base of the run. When the base is not a keep or a
reference (a pinned `interesting` candidate, for example), the bar starts at the nearest kept ancestor of the base. The
bar moves to each keep that the run makes. A run that starts from the head has the head as its bar. Each keep on the
trunk beats the head before it, so the trunk is a chain of improvements. A branch keep that a trunk run merges is not
its bar: the merge must still beat the head.

## The verdict

The daemon gives each measured candidate one verdict (`candidates.verdict`). Until then, the verdict is NULL: the
candidate is **pending**.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `keep` | The primary metric beat the bar with confidence, and every guardrail held. On the trunk, the candidate becomes the new head. |
| `interesting` | The primary metric improved, but without enough confidence, or a guardrail got worse by more than its margin. |
| `discard` | The primary metric did not improve, or a guardrail went past its floor. |
| `crash` | The candidate has no successful measurement: the evaluate command failed or timed out, wrote a bad per-unit results file, or did not print every metric in `piloop.toml`. |

Only the `baseline` and the commits of a `run` are judged. The **origin** of a candidate (`candidates.origin`) says
where it comes from. Three origins never get a verdict:

| Origin | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `reference` | A starting point imported with `piloop candidate import`. |
| `skipped` | A commit marked `Piloop-Eval: skip`. It is never measured, and says nothing about its idea. |
| `recovered` | What an implementer left uncommitted when it stopped, committed by the daemon (`Piloop-Recovered: run#<id>`). The implementer never declared it ready, so it is never measured or kept. If its run did not succeed, the next attempt starts from it, as from the last commit of any such run ([Ideas and runs](/concepts/ideas-and-runs)). |

A later commit of a `skipped` or `recovered` tree without its mark is measured and judged, and its origin becomes
`run`.

The rule, in order:

1. No value for the primary metric: `crash`.
2. A guardrail past its `floor`: `discard`.
3. The bar has no score under the current evaluator: `keep`.
4. The primary metric is not better than the bar: `discard`.
5. The primary metric is better, but its worst case at the `confidence` level is not above zero: `interesting`.
6. A guardrail's worst case is worse than the bar by more than its `margin`: `interesting`.
7. Otherwise: `keep`.

Each verdict has a reason. This one is from the enwik8 example campaign:

```text
against candidate#1: bytes 2720458 vs 3688775 (-968317; 95% worst case -968317 over 3 pairs); guardrails: seconds 10.9 vs 0.8467 (+10.05; 95% worst case +10.28 over 3 pairs) within its margin 60
```

The reason names every guardrail past its floor or beyond its margin, whatever rule decided the verdict. So the
reason of a `discard` on the primary metric still shows a large regression of a guardrail:

```text
against candidate#33: bytes 2365922 vs 2365922 (+0; 95% worst case +0 over 3 pairs); guardrails past their margin: seconds 92.37 vs 23.29 (+69.07; 95% worst case +69.61 over 3 pairs) beyond its margin 60
```

A candidate whose primary and guardrail values equal those of its parent has `no-op` in its reason. The change did
not reach what the evaluate command measures.

## Metric roles

- **primary**: the metric to improve. It must beat the bar with confidence for a `keep`.
- **guardrail**: a metric that may get a little worse. `margin` is the largest regression from the bar that is
  allowed, in the metric's own units. `floor` is an optional hard limit on the value itself: at least the floor for
  `higher`, at most the floor for `lower`.
- **watch**: recorded, and never part of the verdict.

A margin applies to each step. Many keeps that each give up a margin can add up to a large regression. A floor stops
that. Only an edit of `piloop.toml` moves a floor.

## Confidence

When the evaluate command writes [per-unit results](/concepts/campaigns#per-unit-results), the verdict uses a paired
bootstrap over the units:

1. Take the units that the candidate and the bar both report, and the difference of each.
2. Resample the units with replacement 4000 times, and sum the differences each time.
3. The worst case is the `1 - confidence` quantile of these sums. With the default `[plugins.verdict_rule] confidence` of
   0.95, it is the 5th
   percentile.

So a gain that comes from three units has the spread of three units, not of the whole test set. The random generator
has a fixed seed, so the same numbers always give the same verdict.

Without per-unit results, a metric that the candidate and the bar measured in **pairs** is compared on the pairs. A
pair is one run of the evaluate command on the candidate and one on the bar, one after the other on one worker, so a
load on the host slows both. The worst case is the `1 - confidence` quantile of the mean pair difference, from the same
bootstrap over the pairs, and the reason says `over 3 pairs`.

Mark a noisy metric, such as a time, with `repeats`. Then the daemon measures each candidate that the metric judges
`repeats` times, each next to a run of its bar, in the order candidate, bar, bar, candidate, candidate, bar:

```toml
[[metrics]]
name = "bytes"
direction = "lower"
role = "primary"

[[metrics]]
name = "seconds"
direction = "lower"
role = "guardrail"
margin = 60
repeats = 3
```

A candidate then costs `2 × repeats` runs of the evaluate command. `piloop candidate remeasure` measures in pairs too.

Without either, or when the two sides report different units, the verdict compares the aggregate values. Any gain
then counts, and the reason says `aggregate`. This is right for a deterministic metric, such as a compressed size.
For a noisy metric, write per-unit results, or measure in pairs.

## Replace the rule

The rule on this page is the built-in verdict rule, `bootstrap`. A plugin can add another verdict rule, and you
select it in `piloop.toml`. For example, the example plugin `examples/plugins/min-gain` adds the rule `min_gain`. It
keeps a candidate only when the candidate beats its bar by at least `min_gain`:

```text
[plugins.verdict_rule]
provider = "min_gain"
min_gain = 100
```

The rest of this page stays the same with any rule: the bar, the head, the merge when the head moves, and the `no-op`
note. [Verdict rules](/architecture/extension-points#verdict-rules) has the details.

## When the head moves during a run

Two runs can start from the same head. When the first one keeps, the head moves. If the second one then beats its own
bar, it is `interesting`, with the cause `moved`.

The daemon then merges the second candidate onto the new head, as a commit with two parents. The merge is a new
candidate of the same run, and the daemon measures and judges it against the new head. A run that started from a
rebased copy of a head (the same tree under another commit) merges only its own changes. If the merge has conflicts,
the daemon files a seed `Compose candidate#N onto the head` instead, once per candidate: a seed you rejected is not
filed again when the candidate is judged again. The seed of a run's later commit supersedes the seeds of the run's
earlier commits that it contains. The ideas that run after the run's idea, and have not started, then run after the
seed: the change comes with it.

## Measure a candidate again

A fault of the host, such as a full disk, can crash a measurement of good code. A load on the host can slow a
candidate that its bar, measured earlier on a quiet host, then beats. The same tree committed again is the same
candidate, so the daemon does not measure it again by itself. Ask for it:

```bash
piloop candidate remeasure 42
```

The daemon measures the candidate in pairs with its bar, 3 times each (`--repeats`), with the current evaluator,
after the head and the bars that runs need. It stops at the first run that fails. A `crash`, `discard` or
`interesting` that the daemon gave is then judged again on these pairs, as its run would have judged it. So is a
`skipped` or `recovered` commit, which gets its first measurement. A keep, and a verdict that someone set, stay. The
baseline and a reference have no bar: they run alone. A pending candidate is refused: its run measures and judges it.

A stopped campaign is measured too, but only what someone asked for, so you can measure a result again when you
review a campaign. A campaign paused with `--evals`, or held by its stop rule, is measured once the hold ends. The
command says when. `piloop eval queue` shows the request as `waiting` at once, and `piloop eval cancel` cancels it.

## The evaluation queue

Each worker runs at most `evaluations` evaluate commands at the same time, across all campaigns: a key of its
[pool](/architecture/leases#capacity). The others wait in the evaluation queue, which `piloop eval queue` lists. A
free slot goes to the campaign with the fewest running evaluations, and in that campaign to its head first (the runs
that start from the head wait for its score), then to the oldest request. So a busy campaign never holds back another
one. The order in which a run measures its own commits does not change.

A tree is evaluated once: a second request for a candidate that waits or runs waits for that evaluation.

If another process kills an evaluation, the daemon runs it once more. The error of a killed evaluation names the
signal, for example `evaluate killed by SIGTERM`. To stop an evaluation, use `piloop eval cancel`, not `pkill -f`.

```bash
piloop eval queue
piloop eval cancel 42
```

`piloop eval cancel` cancels the evaluation of one candidate: a waiting evaluation never starts, and a running one is
stopped. The candidate gets a crashed measurement that names who cancelled it at once, so a candidate of a run is
judged a `crash`, and a daemon restart does not evaluate it again. Like any crash, it is measured again only when
someone asks (`piloop candidate remeasure`).

## Override a verdict

Any agent or human can change a verdict and say why:

```bash
piloop candidate verdict 42 interesting --reason "lucky seed"
```

The daemon never overwrites a verdict that it did not set. The `candidate.verdict` event records the change with the
reason. Only a judgement (`keep`, `interesting`, `discard`, `crash`) is overridden. The head is the newest trunk keep,
so a keep from an older bar would drop what the head kept since. Such a keep goes the way of the daemon's own (see
[When the head moves during a run](#when-the-head-moves-during-a-run)): the candidate is `interesting`, and the daemon
merges it onto the head, then measures and judges the merge there. A keep of a branch run never moves the head.

## Add a measurement by hand

When you ran the evaluation yourself, for example after the daemon's evaluation timed out, record it:

```bash
piloop measurement add 42 --report report.json
```

`report.json` is `{"metrics": {"accuracy": 0.853}, "seconds": 600}`, with the metrics of `piloop.toml`. The
measurement counts under the current evaluator. When the verdict is the daemon's `crash`, `discard` or `interesting`,
or the commit is `skipped` or `recovered`, the daemon judges the candidate again with all its measurements, as its run
would have judged it. A keep stays: runs started from it as their bar, and they must now beat its new
mean. A pending candidate is refused until the daemon has judged it.

## Notes

A result that regressed can still be a good starting point. A **note** keeps it in view:

```bash
piloop candidate note 42 "faster tokenizer, but loses accuracy on long inputs" --follow-up "fix the long-input case"
```

The candidate becomes `interesting`, with the cause `note` (`candidates.cause`) and your reason before its earlier
reason. Each
`--follow-up` files a seed pinned to the candidate. A note is not a keep, and it does not move a bar. An implementer notes its
own commit with a `Piloop-Note: <why>` trailer in the commit message.
