A campaign costs money in two ways: the tokens that the agents use, and what the campaign pays outside piloop, such as
GPU time or paid APIs. piloop measures the first and enforces a limit on both.

## Tokens

When an agent ends, piloop reads the tokens that it used from the harness's own session file. This works for Claude
Code, Codex and pi. piloop writes the totals to `agent_sessions.usage`:

```json
{"input": 120345, "output": 18230, "cache_read": 950112, "cache_write": 40211, "total": 1128898, "turns": 41, "source": "…"}
```

- `piloop status` and `piloop watch` show the tokens of each campaign.
- The kept lineage shows the tokens that each candidate cost.
- `[stop] max_tokens` ends the campaign when the agents of the campaign have used that many tokens in total.

```toml
[stop]
max_tokens = 50_000_000
```

For every harness, `input` is the input that the prompt cache did not serve, and `cache_read` and `cache_write` are
the cached input. `total` is the harness's own count of the session's tokens.

## Stop conditions

The `[stop]` table ends the campaign when one of its conditions holds:

<Stop />

When a condition holds, the campaign becomes `stopped`. The daemon tells the running implementers to commit and
stop, ends them 10 minutes later, and scores what they committed. It drops the queued runs and the requested agents,
and the watchdog's closing review writes the closing report. Without a `[stop]` table, the campaign has no end.

`piloop campaign stop <campaign>` stops a campaign by hand, the same way. `piloop stop` stops the daemon, not the
campaign. To resume a stopped campaign, change its `[stop]` table and run `piloop init`.

These limits are the built-in stop rule, `limits`. A plugin can add another:
[Stop conditions](/architecture/extension-points#stop-conditions) has an example.

## Spend outside piloop

`[spend]` is a hard limit in US dollars on what the campaign costs outside piloop. piloop knows no vendor, so you give
it a command that prints the spend so far:

```toml
[spend]
command = ["uv", "run", "scripts/spend.py"]   # prints one number: the spend so far in USD
limit = 1000                                  # nothing new starts at this spend
notices = [300, 600]                          # each one posts a note to the inbox when crossed
```

The daemon runs the command every `interval` seconds, at the root of the repo, with its own environment: give it its
keys there ([Secrets](/guides/secrets)). The [configuration reference](/reference/configuration#spend) lists the keys.

The campaign is **held** while the spend is at `limit` or above, while the command fails, and before it first prints
a spend: an unknown spend is never read as zero. At the limit, and for each series of failures, the daemon posts one
escalation at priority 0. While the campaign is held, the daemon starts no agent and no evaluation for it. Agents and
evaluations that already run finish, and their commits are scored.

A hold is not a stop: nothing is dropped. The hold ends on the first tick after the cause ends. To resume after the
limit, raise `limit` in `piloop.toml` and run `piloop init`. After a failure, fix the command; it runs again every
`interval`. A `[stop]` limit does not stop a held campaign.

`piloop campaign stop` stops a held campaign on the next tick. `closing_review` in `[spend]` says what happens to the
closing review of that stop:

| `closing_review` | What the daemon does |
|---|---|
| `"run"` (default) | It starts the closing review, also past the limit or with an unknown spend. |
| `"skip"` | It starts no closing review. The stop ends at once, and the daemon posts an escalation that says so. |
| `"wait"` | It requests the closing review, and starts it when the hold ends. It goes on reading the spend until then. |

A paused campaign that you stop starts its closing review at once: a pause does not hold a stopped campaign.

`piloop status` shows the spend as `$412.30/$1,000.00 spend`, and why the campaign is held:
`$1,012.00/$1,000.00 spend (launches held: spend limit reached)`. Tokens are not part of the spend, because piloop has no
price table. Use `[stop] max_tokens` for them.
